How to refuse or say no in French (beyond 'non merci')

Saying no in French isn't one move — it runs from a gracious decline at the table, through a flat “I can't” or a shrugged “can't be bothered,” to an uncompromising “out of the question.” French reaches for a different phrase at each point. Grouped below by what you're actually doing — and weighted toward what turns up most in real spoken dialogue:

Sans façon  (a sincere “no thank you” — no need to insist)

"— Encore un peu de gâteau ? — Sans façon, merci, c'était délicieux." /sɑ̃ fa.sɔ̃/

“— A little more cake? — No, honestly, thank you — it was delicious.”


Literally “without ceremony.” It signals your no is final and heartfelt, heading off the polite “oh go on, just a little” back-and-forth. Classic at the table; slightly genteel and a touch old-fashioned — which is its charm.


⚠️ The same words used adjectivally mean “unpretentious / no-frills”: un repas sans façon = a no-fuss meal.


Non merci / Non, ça va, merci  (the everyday polite decline — the dinner-table workhorse)

"— Tu veux des œufs brouillés ? — Non, merci, ça va."

“— Want some scrambled eggs? — No, thanks, I'm fine.”


Here ça va means “I'm fine / I'm good as I am,” not “how are you.” Heavily used for food, drink, a cigarette. A near-twin, ça ira, merci, waves off a top-up or extra help (“I'll manage”). ⚠️ A bare Merci to an offer is ambiguous — add non to be unmistakable.


Je peux pas / C'est impossible  (“I can't” — the practical decline, usually with a reason)

"— Tu viens ? — Je peux pas."

“— Are you coming? — I can't.”


Probably the single most common decline in real speech — it refuses on grounds of ability or logistics, not distaste, so it rarely offends. Usually trails an excuse (je peux pas, j'ai un truc). Note the dropped ne: spoken je peux pas vs. written je ne peux pas.


Je vais m'en passer  (“I'll do without / I'll pass”)

"Le dessert a l'air riche — je vais m'en passer."

“The dessert looks rich — I'll pass on it.”


From se passer de (to do without). Neutral — states your choice without judging the offer.


Une autre fois (peut-être)  (“another time, perhaps” — deferring)

"— On prend un café ? — Une autre fois, là je suis débordé."

“— Fancy a coffee? — Another time — I'm swamped right now.”


A soft no dressed as a rain check. Genuine postponement or gentle brush-off — the tone decides.



J'ai pas envie / Pas envie  (“I don't feel like it”)

"J'ai pas envie d'en parler."

“I don't feel like talking about it.”


A refusal grounded purely in mood or desire — no logic, no excuse. That honesty makes it either refreshingly direct or faintly sulky, depending on tone. Takes de + infinitive or noun (j'ai pas envie de le voir). Dropped ne; full form je n'ai pas envie.


Ça m'intéresse pas  (“I'm not interested / it doesn't appeal to me”)

"— Je te propose un CDI. — Non, ça m'intéresse pas."

“— I'm offering you a permanent contract. — No, I'm not interested.”


Neighbour to j'ai pas envie, but the axis differs: j'ai pas envie is about your mood, ça m'intéresse pas about the thing's appeal. Blunt if said flat to someone's proposal — the standard brush-off for a sales pitch or an unwanted offer. Dropped ne; full form ça ne m'intéresse pas. Flip it and ça m'intéresse = count me in.


C'est pas la peine / Pas la peine  (“there's no need / don't bother”)

"— Je vais t'expliquer. — C'est pas la peine."

“— I'll explain. — There's no need.”


La peine = the trouble/effort. You decline help, an explanation, or an effort as unnecessary — gentle (“don't trouble yourself”) or curt (“don't bother”) by tone. Contrast sans façon: that says your no is sincere; pas la peine says the effort is pointless.



Pas question  (clipped, conversational “out of the question”)

"Sortir par ce temps ? Pas question."

“Go out in this weather? Out of the question.”


A trimmed il n'est pas question (de) — conversational and emphatic.


Hors de question  (neutral-standard; fine in formal settings)

"Hors de question que tu leur en fasses cadeau." /ɔʁ də kɛs.tjɔ̃/

“No way you're giving it to them as a gift.”


Same meaning as pas question, a register up — usable in writing and at work. ⚠️ Grammar worth banking: hors de question and pas question take que + subjunctive (que tu fasses, not que tu fais), or de + infinitive. Full emphatic form: il n'en est pas question.


C'est non  (a plain, final “the answer is no”)

"Clem, c'est non !"

“Clem, the answer's no!”


Names the refusal as a settled decision — abrupt and final. Often what a parent or a manager says once discussion is over.


Absolument pas / Certainement pas  (“absolutely not / certainly not”)

"— Tu lui as donné mon numéro ? — Absolument pas."

“— Did you give him my number? — Absolutely not.”


Flat, unambiguous denials of a suggestion or accusation. Neutral — clear without being crude.


Rien du tout  (“nothing at all” — refusing even a fraction)

"Je ne donne rien du tout."

“I'm not giving a single thing.”


An intensifier that slams ne… rien shut — “not one bit.” It also latches onto a bare negation for emphasis: je suis obligée de rien du tout (“I'm not obliged to do anything at all”). Neutral-to-casual spoken.



N'y pense même pas  (“don't even think about it”)

"Emprunter ma voiture ? N'y pense même pas."

“Borrow my car? Don't even think about it.”


Pre-emptive — refuses the idea before the person can act on it. Direct, but not vulgar.


Compte pas sur moi  (“don't count on me” — refusing to take part)

"Compte pas sur moi pour Saber."

“Don't count on me for Saber.”


Personal: I'm out, I won't be part of it. Distinct from n'y compte pas below, which kills an expectation rather than bowing out. Takes pour + noun or infinitive. Full form ne compte pas sur moi.


N'y compte pas  (“don't count on it”)

"Si tu attends des excuses, n'y compte pas."

“If you're waiting for an apology, don't count on it.”


Refuses by killing the expectation rather than the request.


En aucun cas  (“under no circumstances” — formal)

"Ces données ne doivent en aucun cas être partagées."

“This data must under no circumstances be shared.”


Soutenu / administrative — the register of rules, contracts, and official notices.



Même pas en rêve  (“not even in your dreams”)

"Moi, m'excuser ? Même pas en rêve."

“Me, apologize? Not even in my dreams.”


Colloquial and a little cheeky — friends, family, peers, not the workplace.


Jamais de la vie  (“never / not on your life”)

"— Tu remonterais sur ce manège ? — Jamais de la vie !"

“— Would you go on that ride again? — Never in a million years!”


Emphatic and expressive, but not rude — safe in most casual settings.


Tu peux toujours courir  (“you can keep dreaming” — lit. “you can always run”)

"Il veut que je travaille gratuitement ? Il peut toujours courir."

“He wants me to work for free? He can keep dreaming.”


Familiar and a bit mocking. Often third-person (il/elle peut toujours courir).


Compte là-dessus  (ironic “yeah, right — don't hold your breath”)

"— Il a dit qu'il rembourserait. — Compte là-dessus…"

“— He said he'd pay it back. — Yeah, don't hold your breath…”


Read with irony, it means the opposite of “count on it.” Full folk version: compte là-dessus et bois de l'eau (fraîche).



Pas moyen (de…)  (“there's no way to…”)

"Pas moyen d'ouvrir cette fichue porte." /pɑ mwa.jɛ̃/

“No way to get this blasted door open.”


Its center of gravity is an obstacle, not your will — something can't be done, rather than you declining. It can bear on a person (pas moyen de le faire changer d'avis), but the flavor is always “it won't work.”


Il n'y a pas moyen  (fuller, slightly more formal)

"Il n'y a pas moyen de le raisonner."

“There's no reasoning with him.”


⚠️ The English-speaker's trap: “No way!” covers both a refusal (pas question) and a plain reaction of disbelief (c'est pas vrai !, mon œil — see the Disbelief page). Answering “wow, really?!” with hors de question sounds broken.



À retenir

In real dialogue the plain ones win: je peux pas and non, ça va, merci to decline practically, j'ai pas envie, ça m'intéresse pas and c'est pas la peine to beg off, pas question / hors de question to refuse flatly (the second survives in formal settings). The colorful nos — même pas en rêve, tu peux toujours courir — are real but rarer; save them for effect. One systemic note visible right across the attested examples: spoken French drops the ne (je peux pas, j'ai pas envie, compte pas sur moi) — restore it in writing. And keep pas moyen apart: it's impossibility, not refusal.
