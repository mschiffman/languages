FRENCH ORAL VOWELS

A Guide to Oral Vowel Pronunciation.

** Click on the down arrows to see the texts below:

🗣️

French has 12 oral vowel sounds

  • Oral vowels are produced without nasal resonance
French oral vowels are “pure” (monophthongs)
  • They do not glide toward another vowel the way English diphthongs do. For example:
    • beau = /bo/ (stable from start to finish)
    • bow (English) = /bəʊ/ (a glide)
Accent marks affect pronunciation
  • é, è, ê, ë all represent different sounds or contexts
Vowel combinations often create single sounds
  • au, eau, eu, œu create distinct vowel sounds

/a/ - Front A (like "cat" in English)

  • chat, table, avec
  • Most common A sound in modern French

/ɑ/ - Back A (deeper, like "father")

  • pâte, âge
  • Appears with â (circumflex)
  • In modern French, the vowel /ɑ/ has largely merged with /a/.

/e/ - Closed E (like "ay" in "day" without glide)

  • é: café, été
  • e + single consonant: nez
  • -er, -ez: parler, nez
  • -es (grammatical words): les, des

/ɛ/ - Open E (like "bed" in English)

  • è, ê, ë: père, fête, Noël
  • e + double consonant: belle, terre
  • e + final consonant: sec, net
  • ai, ei: maison, neige

/ə/ - Schwa (unstressed, like "the")

  • je, le, de
  • Often dropped in rapid speech

/i/ - Like "ee" in "see" (but shorter and tenser)

  • i, î, ï: si, île, maïs
  • y: lycée, cycle

Important: /i/ & /ɛ̃/

  • In French, “in / im” are pronounced /ɛ̃/ when n or m is followed by another consonant (b, p, t, d, c, g, etc.). When n or m is followed by a vowel, the vowel /i/ is pronounced.
    • i + m/n + a different consonant - important /ɛ̃pɔʀtɑ̃/
    • i + m/n + vowel - inondation /inɔ̃dasjɔ̃/

/o/ - Closed O (like "go" without the glide)

  • ô: hôtel, côte
  • au, eau: auto, beau
  • final -o: vélo, métro

/ɔ/ - Open O (like "caught" in American English)

  • o + consonant: porte, sol, homme

Lips should be rounded and protruded for both sounds

/y/ - This sound doesn't exist in English!

  • u, û: tu, sûr

How to pronounce it:

  • Say "ee" (/i/) and hold it
  • Round your lips forward (like saying "oo")
  • Keep the tongue position of "ee"

Common mistake:

  • ❌ Don't say "oo" like in "boot"
  • ✅ Say "ee" with rounded lips

/u/ - Like "oo" in "boot" (but shorter and tenser)

  • ou, où, oû: vous, où, coûter

Key difference from English:

  • Keep lips strongly rounded throughout
  • No gliding or relaxation
  • More tension than English "oo"

Compare:

  • tu /y/ vs tout /u/
  • du /y/ vs doux /u/

/ø/ - Closed vowel EU (doesn't exist in English)

  • eu at word end: peu, bleu, heureuse
  • eu in open syllables: deux, heureux
  • -euse (final): heureuse /øʀøz/
  • œu (similar context): vœu

/œ/ - Open EU (doesn't exist in English)

  • eu in closed syllables: peur, neuf, jeune
  • œu + consonant: cœur, sœur, œuf

How to pronounce:

  • Say /e/ (closed) and round lips → /ø/
  • Say /ɛ/ (open) and round lips → /œ/
“eu” and “œu” are pronounced /ø/ in open syllables (ending in a vowel sound) and /œ/ in closed syllables (ending in a consonant sound)

/wa/ - Like "wa" in "watch"

  • oi, oy: moi, trois, royal

Common words:

  • moi (me)
  • toi (you)
  • roi (king)
  • boire (to drink)
  • voiture (car)

Note: This is technically a semi-vowel + vowel combination

/j/ - Like "y" in "yes"

  • i + vowel: piano, bien
  • -il, -ille: travail, famille
  • y + vowel: yeux

/ɥ/ - Like /y/ but quicker (no English equivalent)

  • u + vowel: huit, nuit, cuisine

/w/ - Like "w" in "water"

  • ou + vowel: oui, Louis
  • oi: moi, toi (= /wa/)
Many common "-ille" words do not have a /j/ as in "ville", "mille", and "tranquille".

PRO-TIPS & COMMON MISTAKES

Master these key differences between French and English vowels:

  • Don't glide:
    • English vowels often glide (day = /deɪ/). French vowels stay pure throughout.
  • Lip rounding matters:
    • French uses lip position more distinctively than English. Round lips for /y/, /u/, /ø/, /œ/, /o/.
  • Tension and precision:
    • French vowels are more tense and precise than English. Keep muscles engaged.
  • The /y/ sound:
    • Most difficult for English speakers. Practice: "ee" with rounded lips. Say "tu" NOT "too"!
  • Y is both a vowel and a consonant in French:
    • Between two vowels, it is pronounced as the consonant /j/, as in voyager.
  • Closed vs Open:
    • Pay attention to closed (/e/, /o/, /ø/) vs open (/ɛ/, /ɔ/, /œ/) distinctions - they change meaning!
🎯 MINIMAL PAIRS - PRACTICE DISTINCTIONS
  • /i/ vs /y/: si (if) vs su (known), dit (says) vs du (of the)
  • /y/ vs /u/: tu (you) vs tout (all), vu (seen) vs vous (you)
  • /e/ vs /ɛ/: mes (my) vs mais (but), et (and) vs est (is)
  • /o/ vs /ɔ/: beau (beautiful) vs bonne (good), paume (palm) vs pomme (apple)
  • /ø/ vs /œ/: peu (little) vs peur (fear), deux (two) vs d'eux (of them)
