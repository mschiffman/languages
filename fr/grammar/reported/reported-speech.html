French grammar · Reported speech

Il m'a dit que…

Passing on what someone said: « Je suis fatigué » becomes Il m'a dit qu'il était fatigué. The verb shifts back in time, the pronouns change, and questions lose their question form. Here are the rules, and how people actually report speech when they're telling a story.

First, the chart: how the verb shifts

When the reporting verb is in the past (il a dit que, elle m'a expliqué que), the verb in the reported part moves one step back. With il dit que in the present, nothing moves.

Questions lose their question form

Inside a sentence, a question takes plain statement order: no est-ce que, no inversion, no question mark.

Time and place words, when the moment has passed

If you are reporting days or weeks later, words like demain and ici no longer fit. In conversation, people only change them when the original word would be confusing.

Still true? Speech often keeps the present: Il m'a dit qu'il est malade, if he still is. The shifted form is always correct.

Orders: de + infinitive. « Viens ! » → Il m'a dit de venir. No que, no tense to shift.

Pronouns follow the people. « Je t'aime » → Il m'a dit qu'il m'aimait.

The classic trap

Almost the same report, not the same message

De or que, si or de, one tense or another: small choices that change what you are passing on.
