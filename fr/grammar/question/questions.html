Tu viens ?

The textbook asks Viens-tu ? Friends ask Tu viens ?: the same words as a statement, with the voice going up at the end. French has three ways to ask almost anything. Here is which one to use, and how people really ask where, when, what and who.

First, the chart: one question, three ways

Read across a row. All three columns ask the same thing; what changes is who you are talking to. Most of what you hear in daily life is in the first column.

Word Everydayfriends, family, most talk Neutralest-ce que · safe with anyone Carefulinversion · formal, written English

Slide the chart sideways to see every column.

Everyday. Keep the word order of a statement. The question word goes where the answer would go, or at the front: Tu vas où ? · Où tu vas ?

Neutral. Est-ce que at the front never sounds wrong. A good default with strangers, in shops and at work.

Careful. Verb before pronoun, with a hyphen. Mostly written, but a few set phrases use it every day: Comment allez-vous ?

The classic trap

Almost the same question, not the same meaning

One small change, a qui for a que, a word added, or the voice going down instead of up, and you are asking something else.
