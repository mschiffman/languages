Je sais pas

The textbook says Je ne sais pas. The street says Je sais pas, or just Chais pas. In everyday French the ne has all but vanished, and the second word does the whole job. Here is how people really say no, never, nothing and nobody.

First, the chart: the negative words and their positive twins

Written French builds a negative from two parts: ne before the verb and a second word after it. Spoken French keeps only the second word. Each one also has a positive twin, and it helps to learn them as a pair: déjà ↔ pas encore.

Speaking. Dropping ne is not sloppy. Everyone does it in conversation, at every level of society.

Writing. Keep the ne in anything more formal than a text message: Je ne sais pas.

One per verb. Pas never joins rien, personne, jamais. It is J'ai rien vu, not “j'ai pas rien vu”.

The classic trap

Almost the same words, not the same meaning

In each pair the negative words are nearly identical. The order, or one small word, changes what is being denied.
