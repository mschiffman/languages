En fait… du coup…

Linking words turn short sentences into real speech: Il pleuvait, du coup on est restés, mais bon, c'était sympa quand même. Spoken and written French use quite different sets. Here are both, grouped by the job they do, with the ones that change the verb after them.

Each row is one job. The middle columns show what you'll hear in conversation and what you'll read in an article or a formal email. Using the written ones in speech sounds stiff; using du coup in an essay sounds chatty.

More on the subjunctive →

Clause or noun? Many come in pairs: parce que + a sentence, à cause de + a noun; pendant que / pendant.

Same person? Use the infinitive form: avant de partir, pour comprendre, sans dire.

Fillers count. En fait, bon, bref, genre carry no meaning of their own, but they make you sound fluent.

The classic trap

Almost the same word, not the same job

A que added, one letter changed, or a different tone of voice, and the link means something else.
