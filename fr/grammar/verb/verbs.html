French grammar · Verbs

être, avoir, aller, faire

Four verbs carry a huge share of everyday French. They tell your age (j'ai trente ans), the weather (il fait beau), how you are (ça va) and what you're about to do (je vais manger). Here they are, with the verb groups, the spoken shortcuts and the verbs that don't work like their English twins.

First, the chart: the present of the verbs you use most

The four big irregulars, then one model from each regular group, plus prendre. The shaded row is on: in conversation it replaces nous almost every time, so that is the “we” form you will use most.

Slide the chart sideways to see every column.

Fewer sounds than letters. Parle, parles, parle, parlent all sound the same. For an -er verb you only hear three different forms.

On for “we”. It takes the il form: on va, on est, on fait. Easier, and what everyone says.

One present for two. Je mange is both “I eat” and “I'm eating”. French has no separate “-ing” form.

The classic trap

One English verb, two French ones

English uses “know”, “can”, “visit” and “miss” for jobs that French splits between two verbs or two structures.
