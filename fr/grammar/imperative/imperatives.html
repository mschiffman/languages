French grammar · Imperatives

Attends, regarde !

The imperative is the verb with no subject: Écoute, Allons-y, Entrez. French uses it for orders, but even more for small everyday nudges: Tiens (here you go), Allez (come on), T'inquiète (don't worry). Here is how to form it, where the pronouns go, and how to keep it polite.

First, the chart: three forms, no subject

Take the present-tense tu, nous and vous forms and drop the pronoun. Two changes: -er verbs (and aller) lose the -s of the tu form, and four verbs borrow their forms from the subjunctive.

No -s for -er. It's Parle !, Mange !, Va !, but the -s comes back before y and en: Vas-y !

Pronouns after, with hyphens. Dis-moi, Lève-toi, Donne-le-moi. In a negative they go back in front: Ne me dis pas.

Soften it. Add s'il te plaît, or turn it into a question: Tu peux fermer la porte ? A bare imperative to a stranger can sound rude.

The classic trap

Almost the same command, not the same meaning

One small word added, a pronoun or a -y, and the command says something else.
