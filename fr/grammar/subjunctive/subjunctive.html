Il faut que j'y aille

The subjunctive is not a tense but a mood. It switches on after words of needing, wanting, feeling and doubting, and you hear it every day: Il faut que j'y aille, “I've got to go”. Here is what brings it in, what doesn't, and the handful of forms that actually sound different.

First, the chart: what switches it on

The subjunctive almost always comes after que, in the second half of a sentence. Whether it appears depends on the first half: is the speaker stating a fact, or wanting, feeling or doubting something?

How to build it

Take the ils form of the present, drop -ent, and add -e, -es, -e, -ent. For nous and vous, add -ions, -iez to the nous stem of the present, which is why prendre gives que je prenne but que nous prenions.

Two people. It appears when the person wanting or feeling is not the person doing: Je veux que tu viennes.

One person. Use an infinitive instead: Je veux venir, avant de partir.

Often silent. For most -er verbs, que je parle sounds exactly like je parle.

The classic trap

Same verb, two moods

In each pair the first half of the sentence changes slightly, and the verb after que flips between the ordinary indicative and the subjunctive.
