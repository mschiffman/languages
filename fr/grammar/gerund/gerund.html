French grammar · Gerund

en marchant

En + a verb in -ant means “while doing”, “by doing” or “on doing”: Je chante en me douchant. It's one of the few places French uses an -ant form. Most English “-ing” words turn into something else entirely, and that's where the real traps are.

First, the chart: how to build it

Take the nous form of the present, drop -ons, add -ant, and put en in front. It never changes for person, gender or number. Only three verbs break the rule.

What en + -ant can mean

One form, several English translations. The context tells you which. In every case the person doing the -ant verb is the subject of the main verb.

Same subject. “Leaving the house, it started to rain” works loosely in English. In French the -ant verb must belong to the subject.

Never changes. En parlant is the same for je, elle, nous, ils. Pronouns go between: en le voyant, en me levant.

Spoken and written. The gerund with en is everyday French. The bare -ant form without en is mostly written (section 12).
