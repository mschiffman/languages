On m'a volé mon vélo

French has a passive (Mon vélo a été volé), but in conversation people usually avoid it. They say on, se faire, or a reflexive verb instead. Here is how the passive is built, where you'll meet it, and the everyday ways around it.

Building it: être in any tense + past participle

Put être in the tense you need and add the past participle, which agrees with the subject like an adjective. The person who does the action, if you mention them, comes after par.

Tense Active Passive

Agreement. The participle matches the subject: la voiture a été réparée, les clés ont été retrouvées.

“I was told” doesn't work. Only a direct object can become the subject. For “I was told, given, asked”, use on: On m'a dit.

Spoken French prefers on. On parle français ici sounds natural; Le français est parlé ici sounds like a sign.
