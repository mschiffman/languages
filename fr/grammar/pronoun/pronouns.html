French grammar · Pronouns

je, me, moi, le mien

English has “I, me, my, mine”. French has a few more forms, puts most of them before the verb, and in everyday speech says on far more often than nous. Here is the whole family on one chart, then each form the way people really use it.

First, the chart: one person, seven forms

Read across a row to see every form one person can take. Read down a column to see one job. Choose a person to see a sentence for each form. The colours are the same all the way down the page.

Slide the chart sideways to see every column.

Short forms. Before a vowel, je, me, te, se, le, la lose their vowel: j'ai, il m'aide, je t'aime, je l'ai vu.

Where it differs. Only the third person has three different object forms: le / lui / lui and les / leur / eux. For me, te, nous, vous one form does it all.

Mine, yours. The last column changes with the thing owned: le mien, la mienne, les miens, les miennes.
