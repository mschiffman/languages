French grammar · Prepositions

à, de, en, chez

English says “in” Paris, “in” France, “in” the box and “in” May. French says à Paris, en France, dans la boîte and en mai. The small words rarely match one to one, so here they are the way people actually use them, with the traps marked.

à and de swallow le and les

This always happens, in speech and in writing. La and l' stay as they are.

à + leauau cinéma

à + lesauxaux toilettes

de + ledule livre du prof

de + lesdesla fin des vacances

Unchanged: à la gare · à l'école · de la mer · de l'hôtel

“In” and “to” a place: pick the word by the kind of place

The same word means both “in” and “to”: J'habite à Lyon, Je vais à Lyon.

The place is… Word In a sentence

No word-for-word. Learn each preposition with the noun or verb it goes with: penser à, en voiture, chez moi.

Never at the end. “Who are you talking to?” is Tu parles à qui ?: the preposition stays with its word.

Sometimes none at all. Chercher, attendre, écouter already contain the English “for”, “for”, “to”.

The classic trap

One small word, a different meaning

In each pair only the preposition changes. English often needs a whole different sentence to show the difference.
