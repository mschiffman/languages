plus, moins, aussi, mieux

English has “-er”, “more”, “less”, “as … as”. French builds almost everything with three small words and que. The catch is that the pattern shifts with what you compare: a quality, a quantity or an action. Here is how people compare things in everyday spoken French.

First, the chart: what are you comparing?

Choose the row by the kind of word you compare, then read across. Two things to notice: “as” is aussi with a quality but autant with a quantity or an action, and nouns need de.

The ones that don't use plus

Four everyday words have their own “better / worse / more / less”. Meilleur is an adjective and agrees; mieux is an adverb and never changes.

And the sound of « plus »

Examples with plus carry one of these tags. The « plus » page has the full story.

/ply/
Silent s: before an adjective or adverb that starts with a consonant. plus grand, plus vite

/plyz/
Linking z: before a vowel sound. plus‿important, de plus‿en plus

/plys/
You hear the s: “more” as an amount. plus de temps, il gagne plus
