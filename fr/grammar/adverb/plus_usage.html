The many uses of « plus »

One little word, ten jobs: “no more”, “more”, “-er”, “the most”, “and on top of that”… Here is how people really use it in everyday spoken French.

First, the sound: three ways to say it

In casual speech the ne of ne… plus is usually dropped, so the only thing that tells “no more” from “more” is whether you hear the final s. Every example below carries one of these tags.

/ply/

Silent s

Negative “no more / no longer”, and “more” in front of an adjective or adverb that starts with a consonant.

/plys/

You hear the s

“More” as a quantity, on its own or at the end of the phrase, in sums, and in en plus.

/plyz/

Linking z

“More” in front of an adjective or adverb that starts with a vowel sound: plus‿important.

The classic trap

Same words, opposite meanings

Without ne, these sentences are written exactly the same. In speech, the s decides. In writing, add ne for the negative, or use davantage / encore for “more”.
