French grammar · Nouns

un livre, une table

Every French noun is masculine or feminine, even a table or an idea, and you usually can't hear the plural at all: le livre and les livres differ only in the little word in front. Here is how to guess the gender, build the plural, and put nouns together the French way.

Plurals: mostly a silent -s

In writing, almost every noun adds -s or -x. In speech you hear the plural on the article (le → les, un → des), not on the noun, except in the few rows marked below.

Rule One Several Watch out

Learn it with un or une. L' hides the gender: learn une idée, un arbre, not l'idée, l'arbre.

Gender spreads. It changes the article, the adjective and the pronoun: la petite table, je la prends.

Not about the thing. Gender is grammar, not meaning: une personne is feminine even when it's a man.

The classic trap

Same word, other gender, other meaning

A handful of nouns exist in both genders with different meanings. Only le or la tells you which one is meant.
