The five vowels

Before the vowels, a quick map of the whole system — Japanese is written with three scripts working together, and it helps to know where you are.

The three writing systems

ScriptWhat it's forExample
Hiragana ひらがな native Japanese words and grammar ありがとう — thank you
Katakana カタカナ foreign and loan words, emphasis コーヒー — coffee
Kanji 漢字 meaning-based characters, adopted from Chinese 水 — water　本 — book

One ordinary sentence usually weaves all three together:

私はコーヒーを飲みます。

I drink coffee.

Kanji 私・飲 Hiragana は・を・みます Katakana コーヒー

This course begins with hiragana — the script for native words and grammar, and the one you sound out. Its first five characters are the vowels at the top of this page: あ い う え お. Every Japanese syllable is built from these five sounds, so they are the natural place to begin.

HiraganaRomajiApproximate sound
あalike the a in father
いilike the ee in see
うua short, light u — lips relaxed, not the rounded oo of English
えelike the e in bed
おoa short, clean o
あ　い　う　え　お
a — i — u — e — o

This order never changes. You will meet it again and again — in dictionaries, in verb tables, in the layout of the full kana chart — so it is worth fixing in memory as a little chant: a-i-u-e-o.

Notice something useful straight away: Japanese spelling is far more regular than English. Once you know the kana, you can usually look at a word and know exactly how it is pronounced.

One habit to build early — keep each vowel pure and steady. English vowels tend to glide: the o in go slides toward oo, the a in day toward ee. Japanese vowels do not. Say each one as a single, clean sound, then stop.

You can already read real words

  • あい ai love
  • いえ ie house, home
  • うえ ue above, on top
  • あお ao blue
  • いい ii good

Writing the kana

Japanese is written with a fixed stroke order. Two rules cover most of it: top to bottom, and left to right (a horizontal stroke usually comes before the vertical one that crosses it). Following the standard order keeps your writing legible now and makes it much faster later.

あ
3 strokes
い
2 strokes
う
2 strokes
え
2 strokes
お
3 strokes

Copy each one several times, keeping to the order above.

Reading test

Read each one aloud first, then tap the card to check.

A note on いえ (ie). Don't let English pull it into “eye-ee.” The two vowels stay separate and clean: i – e. This holds whenever vowels sit side by side in Japanese — each one keeps its own clear sound.
