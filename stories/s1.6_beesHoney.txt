Buzz! Buzz! Buzz!
Look at the little bees!
They are flying
from flower to flower.
But what are they doing?
How do bees make honey?
Let's find out!
Flowers make a sweet liquid
called nectar.
Bees love nectar!
A bee lands on a flower
and drinks the nectar
with its long tongue.
Then it flies
to another flower.
Where does the nectar go?
The bee carries it
in a special part of its body
called a honey stomach.
This is different from the stomach
it uses to digest its own food.
The bee carries the nectar
back to its hive.
The bee returns home.
Inside the hive,
many other bees are working.
The bee passes the nectar
to another bee.
The bees add special substances
called enzymes
that help change the nectar.
They work together
to make honey!
Nectar has a lot of water in it.
Bees put the nectar
into little honeycomb cells.
They fan their wings
to move air through the hive.
Slowly, water evaporates.
The nectar becomes
thick, sweet honey!
The honey is ready!
The bees cover the full honeycomb cells
with a thin layer of wax.
This keeps the honey safe.
Bees store honey to eat
when flowers are hard to find.
Their hard work
helps the whole colony.
How do bees make honey?
First, they collect nectar
from flowers.
Then they bring it home
and change it.
They remove water
until it becomes thick honey.
Finally, they store it
in the honeycomb.
What amazing little workers!
